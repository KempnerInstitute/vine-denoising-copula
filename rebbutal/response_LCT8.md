We thank the reviewer for an exceptionally careful reading — the five concerns are exactly the right questions, and we spent the response period running the experiments they call for. All results below are from new runs with the same frozen checkpoint and protocols as the paper.

**Q1 — In-distribution headline; test on families excluded from training.**

We agree Table 1 measures in-distribution amortization quality, and the revision states this plainly. We ran the requested out-of-distribution benchmark: nine bivariate cases from families entirely absent from the training zoo — BB6 (two parameterizations), BB8 (two), Tawn (asymmetric extreme-value), Plackett (θ=5, 20), and 90° rotations of BB6/BB8 — against pyvine-TLL refit per case (n=2000, 3 seeds, same grid and metrics as Table 1):

| metric (suite mean) | VDC (frozen) | pyvine-TLL (per-case refit) |
|---|---|---|
| ISE | 9.61e-5 | 9.58e-5 |
| Kendall-τ error | 0.013 (better in 7/9 cases) | 0.018 |
| MI error (nats) | 0.025 | 0.054 |

So out of distribution the two-orders-of-magnitude ISE gap disappears, as the reviewer predicted — but the frozen estimator still matches a per-case-refit nonparametric baseline on density ISE and is roughly 2× more accurate on dependence summaries (largest gaps on the tail-asymmetric BB6/Tawn/rotated cases). We will state both facts: Table 1 = in-distribution interpolation; OOD = parity-or-better without any refitting.

**Q2 — Matched compute.**

We owe the reviewer a correction that works against our submission's own framing: on re-examining the run records, the pyvine-TLL baselines were fit with vinecopulib's multithreading enabled (8–16 threads, matching allocated cores) — the footnote's "pinned to a single thread" is wrong. The submitted speedups are therefore already GPU-versus-multithreaded-C++. We verified by rerunning the full thread sweep on the same benchmark, seeds, and sample sizes (fit seconds, mean over 3 seeds):

| d | VDC (H100) | TLL 1 thread | TLL 8 threads | TLL 16 threads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 44.1 | 1465.5 | 203.3 | 119.6 |
| 200 | 172.8 | 5897.4 | 775.6 | 426.3 |
| 500 | 1081.6 | >2.2e4 (one fit exceeded a 6.3 h budget) | 4726.0 | 3101.9 |

So the honest statement is: 2.5–4.6× versus multithreaded vinecopulib, 21–34× versus single-thread. Pretraining break-even is ≈478/125/59 vines at d=100/200/500 against the multithreaded baseline (≈46/11 vines against single-thread). For the other matched-compute direction we ran VDC inference on CPU only — same machine, same 8 threads as the TLL baseline, no GPU: **VDC 181.8 s vs TLL 203.3 s at d=100, and 729.8 s vs 775.6 s at d=200** (held-out NLL identical to the GPU fits). So on strictly matched hardware the amortized fit is still faster than multithreaded vinecopulib — the advantage is algorithmic, and the GPU multiplies it (mid-range RTX: 35/146 s; H100: 44/173 s). The revision corrects the footnote and reports all of this.

**Q3 — UCI near the independence floor.**

The reviewer's reading is correct, including "slightly worse than independence." We ran a dependence diagnostic: fitted Gaussian-copula total correlation after the PIT preprocessing is 0.001 (Gas), 0.002 (Hepmass), 0.006 (Credit), 0.017 (Miniboone) nats — versus 1.881 on Power; mean pairwise |τ|≈0.008 versus 0.409. So these four datasets genuinely cannot discriminate copula-space density methods: every structured method sits at the independence floor, and VDC's +0.0002–0.0019 bits/dim is the (small) cost of fitting structure where there is essentially none. The revision reframes the UCI table as "the amortized edge does no harm inside a learned vine," names the floor explicitly, and points to the discriminative settings: the Clayton-vine benchmark and S&P100. On the latter we additionally ran the stricter chronological split (train earliest 80%, test latest 20%; n_train=1356, d=100): VDC −0.419 bits/dim vs pyvine-TLL −0.293, Gaussian copula −0.337, RealNVP −0.047, all paired-bootstrap differences favoring VDC at p<0.001. Notably TLL falls below the trivial Gaussian copula there (overfitting at this n/d), which we read as the pretrained synthetic prior acting as a regularizer — this is where the method genuinely earns its keep on real data.

**Q4 — Self-consistency thinness.**

Agreed on both counts as submitted. We scaled the DPI protocol from 60 to 480 trials per estimator: VDC 10/480 (2.1%) vs KSG 25/480 (5.2%), Fisher exact p=0.015. Because the tolerance τ was also (fairly) questioned, we computed the full sensitivity: VDC has fewer violations at every τ, significantly for τ ∈ {0, 1e-4, 3e-4, 1e-3, 3e-3} (e.g., τ=0: 13 vs 32, p=0.005; τ=1e-3: 10 vs 25, p=0.015); both estimators reach 0 violations only at τ≥0.03 nats. On additivity (0.027 vs 0.044 nats at 480 trials), we concede the reviewer's structural point in part — and tested it. Beyond KSG's dimensional bias, our LHS used a block D-vine encoding the true across-block independence (side information KSG does not receive; we disclose this ourselves). We reran additivity with fully learned Dißmann R-vine structure, all three estimators on identical data per trial (120 paired trials): oracle structure 0.024±0.020 nats, **learned structure 0.024±0.020**, KSG 0.039±0.029. The structure asymmetry therefore does not drive the gap; KSG's dimensional bias plausibly contributes to its residual, and the revision keeps that caveat while reporting the (now significant) DPI contrast as the primary finding.

**Q5 — Conditional-task diagnosis; deep-tree ambiguity.**

We ran the decomposition the reviewer asks for. Replacing the approximate importance-weighted imputation procedure with exact conditional sampling from the fitted vine (per missing pattern, refit the D-vine with observed variables ordered first — affordable precisely because fitting is amortized — then invert the Rosenblatt transform with the observed prefix held fixed) gives: Gas RMSE 1.151→1.001 (best simple baseline 0.993), Credit 1.415→1.018 (best 1.013), Power 6.893→6.897 (kNN 5.743). So on two of three datasets the reported deficit was almost entirely the sampling procedure, not the fitted model; Power's gap persists and is a genuine model/marginal limitation. On IPFP distortion: varying the projection from K=15 to K=100 leaves UCI NLL and PIT-KS unchanged (App. table), and training includes a corner-weighted tail loss, but we agree a formal statement is unavailable — the revision says so. On the deep-tree ambiguity, we ran exactly the requested experiment and report it in full, because it partially confirms the reviewer's hypothesis. We constructed a simplified D-vine (d=30, 29 trees) with Clayton(θ=2) at **every** tree level — true per-edge dependence 0.432 nats at all depths, verified by quadrature, Monte Carlo, and grid integration — an adversarial stress test (conditional τ=0.5 even at tree 29). At n_train=20{,}000, recovered per-edge dependence (true | VDC | TLL, nats): tree 1: 0.437 | 0.429 | 0.432; trees 2–5: 0.431 | 0.352 | 0.347; trees 6–10: 0.432 | 0.030 | 0.000; trees 11+: ≈0 for both estimators. So: both VDC and the classical nonparametric baseline recover deep dependence well through ~tree 5 and attenuate beyond it (VDC no faster — slightly slower — than TLL at every depth). The reviewer is right that near-zero deep-edge NLL cannot, by itself, distinguish "no dependence" from "attenuated estimation"; this attenuation is a shared finite-sample property of simplified-vine pipelines (conditioning-variable estimation error compounds through the recursion), not specific to our estimator. For the UCI datasets specifically, the independence reading is supported by evidence that does not pass through deep-tree estimation: the total Gaussian-copula TC of those datasets is 0.001–0.017 nats (Q3), leaving essentially nothing for any tree to carry. The revision reports this experiment and scopes the depth-stability claim accordingly.

We believe these results resolve Q1–Q4 with data and substantially advance Q5, and we would be glad to run any further variant the reviewer considers decisive.
