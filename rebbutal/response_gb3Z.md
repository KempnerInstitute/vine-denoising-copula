We thank the reviewer for a review that asks exactly the right positioning question — what is gained and what is lost — and answer it directly, with several new experiments run during the response period.

**What is gained relative to fitting a standard nonparametric estimator per edge.**

1. *Accuracy is not sacrificed — including out of distribution.* The natural worry is that a pretrained estimator only wins on its own training families. We ran a new benchmark on nine bivariate cases from families entirely absent from training (BB6, BB8, Tawn, Plackett, and 90° rotations), against pyvine-TLL refit per case: density ISE is at parity (9.61e-5 vs 9.58e-5 suite mean), while the frozen estimator is more accurate on dependence summaries — Kendall-τ error 0.013 vs 0.018 (better in 7/9 cases) and half the MI error (0.025 vs 0.054 nats). In distribution, a new 800-paired-replication study (100 fresh replications per family, both methods on identical samples) gives VDC per-replication win rates of 88% [85, 90] on both ISE and MI error. So the trade is not "speed for accuracy": per-edge accuracy is preserved or improved.
2. *Speed changes what is practical, not just benchmark times.* At d=100/200/500 the fitted-vine wall-clock is 2.5–4.6× below multithreaded vinecopulib (21–34× below single-thread; we also correct a submission footnote here — the baselines were in fact multithreaded, so reported speedups were conservative in framing). This is what makes repeated-fitting workflows feasible: rolling-window refits (our S&P100 dashboard: 289 window refits), per-missing-pattern refits for exact conditional imputation (below), structure sweeps, and repeated information-theoretic analyses.
3. *A regularization benefit at small n.* On S&P100 (n_train=1356, d=100) under a new, stricter chronological split, VDC reaches −0.419 bits/dim while per-edge TLL overfits to −0.293 — below even a single-covariance Gaussian copula (−0.337) — and RealNVP collapses (−0.047); all paired-bootstrap differences p<0.001. The synthetic pretraining prior acts as an edge-level regularizer precisely where flexible per-dataset estimators overfit.
4. *Everything a classical vine supports remains available*, because the fitted object is a classical simplified vine with explicit pair-copula densities and h-functions — this is the point of the design, and a new ablation shows it is load-bearing: disabling the marginal projection (keeping the same network) produces a non-normalized object whose apparent likelihood exceeds the representation's information ceiling ~10× and degrades UCI NLL by 0.43–0.85 bits/dim.

**What is lost.** We state this candidly (and the revision does too): (i) grid discretization — a 64×64 piecewise-constant density caps representable MI at log m ≈ 4.16 nats and cannot resolve tail structure below 1/m; (ii) the per-edge estimator has no consistency/rate theory, unlike kernel estimators — its status matches the amortized-inference class generally; (iii) an 18-hour one-time pretraining cost, amortized only after ≈50–480 vine fits depending on d and baseline threading; (iv) the simplified-vine scope (shared with standard pipelines), where on regime-switching stress tests VDC is the best pooled estimator but a conditional oracle wins by construction.

**"Can the fitted model support simulation, prediction, imputation, tail and higher-tree dependence?"** — point by point, with numbers:

- *Simulation:* yes, via inverse h-functions (appendix algorithm), and now quantified (n_sim=5000, 3 seeds; simulated vs held-out pairwise Kendall-τ, with a bootstrap of real training data as the attainable floor): τ-matrix RMSE — Gas: VDC 0.016 vs TLL 0.017 vs floor 0.018; Power: 0.020 vs 0.036 (floor 0.011); S&P100: 0.047 vs 0.192 (floor 0.039). VDC-simulated data sits essentially at the real-data floor on all three datasets; per-dataset TLL refits degrade ~5× on the hardest case, consistent with the small-n overfitting pattern above.
- *Conditional prediction / imputation:* this was our weakest submitted result, and a new experiment shows the deficit was mostly the evaluation procedure, not the model. Replacing approximate importance-weighted imputation with exact conditional sampling (per missing pattern: refit the D-vine with observed variables first — affordable because fitting is amortized — and invert the Rosenblatt transform with the observed prefix fixed): Gas RMSE 1.151→1.001 (best simple baseline 0.993), Credit 1.415→1.018 (best 1.013); Power remains behind kNN (6.897 vs 5.743) — a genuine limitation we keep stated.
- *Tail dependence:* measured directly — Table 1 reports upper-tail-dependence error (suite mean 0.006 vs 0.081–0.104 for baselines), the training loss upweights corner regions, and the OOD suite above includes tail-asymmetric families (BB6, Tawn) where the dependence-summary advantage is largest. The 1/m resolution floor is the stated boundary.
- *Higher-tree conditional dependence:* partially, and we can now quantify the boundary. On a d=8 controlled vine, per-edge estimated-vs-true MI tracks ground truth across all trees (Fig. 3(a); MAE 0.004 nats; knockout correlation 0.93). A new adversarial stress test — a d=30 vine with strong Clayton dependence (0.432 nats) at *every* one of 29 trees, n=20,000 — shows recovery of 98% (tree 1) and 82% (trees 2–5) of true dependence, attenuating beyond tree ~6; the classical nonparametric baseline attenuates slightly faster (VDC 0.030 vs TLL 0.000 nats at trees 6–10). So deep-tree recovery is a genuine finite-sample boundary of simplified-vine estimation generally — not specific to amortization — and the revision states it.

**Reliability diagnostics for out-of-zoo edges.** Two per-edge signals are observable at inference without ground truth: (i) the pre-projection marginal deviation of the raw network output (large deviation = the network is off-manifold; the IPFP ablation shows what its magnitude means), and (ii) the residual between the input histogram and the predicted density under the fitted grid (a goodness-of-fit statistic). Both are cheap and logged; the revision adds a diagnostics paragraph, and the OOD suite above provides empirical calibration for how the estimator behaves off-zoo (gracefully, in our tests).

**On the name.** We accept the point: this is an amortized estimation procedure for classical simplified vines, not a new model class. The revision's framing (title and contributions) says "amortized pair-copula estimation for simplified vines"; we retain "VDC" as the estimator's name for continuity but are happy to adjust if the reviewer or AC prefers.

We hope this clarifies that the amortization enables tasks that per-edge estimation makes impractical (repeated refits, exact per-pattern conditional imputation, large-d information analysis) while preserving — and out of distribution matching, at small n exceeding — the accuracy of standard per-edge estimators.
